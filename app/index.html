Why averages lie

Open any train site and you will find one number per station: average delay. On etrain's running-history pages that is all there is — an average, and three wide buckets. But a connection is not killed by the average day. It is killed by the bad tail: the one day in eight when the train rolls in three hours late. A train can average 25 minutes late and still end your trip once a month. Parliament's own questions on punctuality name the harm plainly — missed connecting trains, financial losses — and answer it with one network-wide percentage, about 77%. Neither number can tell you whether your buffer is enough.

So this page asks the only question that matters at booking time: on how many of its recorded runs did this train, at this station, arrive later than the time you are leaving? That share is your risk. And it works backwards too: the buffer that would have kept your risk under 5%, counted from the same runs.

How this was checked

The counts come from a year of per-run records (February 2025 – February 2026, 2.7 million station stops after cleaning). To prove the method is not just confident but right, it was tested the strict way: probabilities were computed from runs before September 2025, then scored against what actually happened from September 2025 onwards, across 26,239 train-and-station pairs. Honest limits

  • History, not prophecy. A strike, a derailment or fog on your day is not in last year's counts. This tells you the odds, not the future.
  • Some recorded times are estimates. The openly licensed copy of this data lost the flags that mark which station times were reported and which were filled in by the system. That is stated here, on the page, not buried — and it is why every answer shows its run count.
  • Coverage is not the whole railway. The year's records used here cover 3,060 trains. Some well-known trains (the Howrah Rajdhani, 12301, among them) are missing from the openly licensed copy — if your train is not found, that is a gap in the data, not in your ticket.
  • Few runs, no answer. Below 30 recorded runs for a train at a station, this page refuses to guess.
  • Booking-time tool. It does not show today's running status. For that, use the official NTES enquiry.
