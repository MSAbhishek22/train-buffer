# Train Buffer — Reference Research (24 Nov 2026)

1. **etrain.info running-history pages** (read directly, Nov 2026) —
   the incumbent. Per station it shows exactly one number, "Avg.
   Delay", plus three coarse colour buckets (Right Time 0–15 min,
   Slight 15–60, Significant >1 hr). On the Saharsa–LTT special the
   printed averages run to 700–940 minutes — a number no traveller
   can act on. Takeaway: the gap is not data, it is the *question*.
   Nobody answers "later than MY buffer, how often?"
2. **ConfirmTkt / ixigo train pages** (category knowledge, cross-
   checked against the etrain pattern above) — running status and
   "right time %" buckets; same descriptive-statistics ceiling.
3. **Lok Sabha answer on train punctuality** (sansad.in, answered
   Aug 2026, crawled Nov 2026) — official overall punctuality ~77%
   for Mail/Express in 2025-26, and the question itself names the
   harm: "missed connecting trains, financial losses". Takeaway:
   the connection problem is on the official record; a single
   network-wide percentage is the opposite of a per-train answer.
4. **Google Flights delay notices** (product pattern) — flight UIs
   already speak in risk language ("likely to be delayed"), never
   in raw averages. Takeaway: travellers understand probabilities
   phrased as frequencies — "1 in 8 runs" — better than means.
5. **The printed Indian Railways timetable** (design ancestor) —
   tabular numerals, hairline rules, station rows, zero decoration.
   Takeaway: Train Buffer's visual identity. Numbers set like a
   timetable are instantly trusted by the exact user we serve.
6. **MeetMap (CHI 2025)**, carried over from the BolNote research —
   distributions communicate better as shapes than as tables.
   Takeaway: the survival curve becomes a terrain you can read at a
   glance; the buffer is a wall standing in it.

## What we deliberately do NOT copy
- No live running status (NTES live data is a different product and
  a different licence question). This is a booking-time tool.
- No "AI prediction" framing. The numbers are counts from recorded
  runs, with the count always shown.
